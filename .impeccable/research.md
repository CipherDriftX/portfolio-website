# Research: how paid AI engineers and craft sites earn trust

Checked 2026-11-21 from headless screenshots at 1280x800 (first screen only). Principles only: no layout, colour or copy is taken from any of these.

| Site | Trust in 10 seconds | Principle to take |
|---|---|---|
| hamel.dev | A real face next to a plain sentence with named employers (Airbnb, GitHub) and a specific practice word, "evals". | Name the method you sell in the first paragraph; one specific noun beats a list of skills. |
| jxnl.co | "Jason Liu here" and a current role, then a named library others cite. Reads like a person, not a brochure. | First person, present tense, one line on what you do today. |
| eugeneyan.com | Huge name, one sentence of mission, then a "Latest" list with dated, specific titles. Output is visible on screen one. | Let the work list sit right under the intro so the visitor sees there is more without being told. |

| huyenchip.com | Text-first page in her own voice with a photo; concrete claims (book, past employers) linked inline. | Every claim links to its proof. |

## What this changes for "Night window"

- The people who get paid for AI work win on specifics: a named practice (evals), dated entries, linked proof. None of them use a gradient, a counter or a skill cloud.
- The craft sites win on restraint plus one committed move. For this site that move is the photo and its light; everything else stays small and quiet.
- A section with no entries hurts more than no section. Drafts stay hidden until real.
- The anti-reference is dark too, so dark alone is not the difference. The difference must come from the photo's own warm light (tungsten, umber) instead of a cool navy, and from flat grouping by space instead of cards, pills and nav lines.
